# Vaultito — logo and app icon design brief

Prepared October 18, 2026. Initial use: an English-language iPhone beta distributed through internal TestFlight.

## The assignment

Create an original visual identity for **Vaultito**, including a logo, wordmark, primary iOS app icon and a small family of alternate app icons. We want an identity that feels personal, approachable and carefully made, with enough distinction to recognize on a crowded Home Screen.

The existing app artwork comes from an upstream project and must be replaced. This is a new identity, not a refresh of that artwork.

## Product and audience

Vaultito is a private, local-first photo and video vault. Its direction includes nested albums, user-selected album protection, password and optional Face ID access, and a separate alternative vault opened by a different passcode. Some security and recovery features are still in development; branding must not imply independent certification or absolute protection.

The audience is everyday iPhone users who want control over personal media without a complicated or intimidating security product. The app should feel comfortable holding personal memories, not like enterprise security software or a cryptocurrency wallet.

**Brand name:** Vaultito — use this exact spelling and capitalization.

**Tagline:** Your photos. Your keys. Your rules.

Use the tagline in supporting brand applications where it remains readable. It should not appear inside the app icon and does not need to be part of every logo lockup.

## Personality and visual direction

- Friendly, confident, calm and personal.
- Simple enough to feel trustworthy; distinctive enough to avoid a generic security-app appearance.
- A little warmth or playfulness suits the name. Avoid looking childish or novelty-driven.
- Strong silhouette, deliberate spacing and restrained detail matter more than decorative effects.
- Color is open. Warm coral, plum, tangerine, blue and berry were explored; none is approved or mandatory.

The owner also has apps called **Ghostcheck** and **Omnicast**. Leave room for a future family resemblance through typography, proportions or spacing. Their visual identities have not been supplied, so do not assume a shared palette, mascot or existing design system.

## Logo and wordmark requirements

Develop a standalone symbol and a readable **Vaultito** wordmark that work together and independently. A custom V, a photo-frame detail or a subtle idea of personal space could be useful starting points, but the designer should explore stronger alternatives.

The symbol must work at small sizes, in one color, and on both light and dark backgrounds. The wordmark should feel approachable and mature, with clear Latin lettering. No non-Latin lettering or miniature text as decoration.

Avoid relying on a literal padlock, shield, keyhole, safe dial or “military-grade” visual language. Avoid intricate linework, tiny windows, fine gradients or details that disappear at icon size. Do not use a loop, Möbius strip or folded ivory ribbon on teal: that would be too close to the inherited identity.

## Primary app icon

The primary icon should be the clearest expression of the brand symbol. It needs to remain recognizable at approximately 40–60 points and hold up beside standard iOS icons.

- No wordmark, tagline, initials beyond an intentional abstract monogram, or small text.
- Strong contrast and a clear focal shape; test on light and dark Home Screen wallpapers.
- Compose for the iOS corner mask; do not bake rounded outer corners into the export.
- Check optical weight, safe margins and whether the shape remains distinctive in monochrome.
- Avoid stock-photo treatments, busy collages and superficial resemblance to established app marks.

## Alternate app icons

Present **three alternate icon options** alongside the primary. At least one should be understated and abstract, without an obvious lock/security symbol or private-photo imagery. Offer meaningful differences in shape or treatment, rather than only recoloring the same tile.

The alternates should still feel intentional and part of the same product. Explore a monochrome treatment and a softer or more expressive option if useful. Do not repeat the inherited paper, calculator, pebble or ruler artwork.

Discretion is a visual preference, not a security guarantee. Changing the icon does not hide the installed app, change its name or make an alternative vault undetectable. Do not use messaging or visuals that imply otherwise.

## Requested process and deliverables

**First presentation:** two or three substantially different identity directions. For each, show the symbol, wordmark, primary app icon and a short explanation. Include realistic small-size views and light/dark background examples. Explain which direction you recommend and why.

**Final selected direction:**

- Editable vector master in the designer's native format, plus SVG and PDF exports.
- Standalone symbol, horizontal logo/wordmark lockup, and an optional tagline lockup.
- Full-color, one-color, light-background and dark-background versions; transparent exports for the logo where appropriate.
- Primary icon plus three selected alternates, each as a clean square **1024 × 1024 PNG** with an opaque full-bleed background for the app's current asset pipeline.
- Small-size icon previews, including approximately 40, 60 and 120-pixel views, plus a Home Screen mockup.
- Color values in HEX/RGB, typography and font-license details, minimum size, spacing and basic usage guidance.
- A symbol export suitable for the login/launch screen, so the same identity can replace inherited branding throughout the app.
- Any useful simplified/tinted or layered icon treatment, with editable sources, clearly identified separately from the current PNG deliverables.

Provide final asset names and a short export inventory. Avoid flattening everything into a single presentation image.

## Originality, rights and attribution

Create original artwork. Do not reuse the upstream project's logo, icons, screenshots or brand assets. Avoid stock/template elements unless disclosed and specifically approved. Identify all fonts and third-party materials and their license terms, and disclose any generative AI use.

The final engagement should give the owner the rights needed to use, modify and distribute the artwork in the app, website, marketing and matching open-source release. Flag any restriction that would prevent inclusion in the public source repository. Copyright/trademark clearance is a separate review; neither these concepts nor the designer's presentation should be described as cleared without that work.

Keep upstream software attribution and license notices intact. New visual branding does not replace those obligations.

## What success looks like

Someone can recognize Vaultito from its icon at a glance. The logo feels friendly and credible, works without elaborate effects, and gives us a coherent identity across the Home Screen, login screen and future website. The alternate icons provide genuine choice while staying polished and easy to recognize.
